IX Dark energy & the cold sky

The cosmos, driven from inside time

In the Temporal Framework, dark energy is not an energy of empty space but a decaying temporal density, ρΛ = α₃/t³, whose gradient pushes where gravity pulls. Calibrated at α₃ ≈ 6.08×1026 kg·s³/m³ (ρΛ = 7.36×10−27 kg/m³ today), the correction lifts the late-time Hubble rate onto the SH0ES side of the tension.

H₀ is computed, not asserted: fed the CMB's H₀-independent physical matter density, the framework's ρΛ = α₃/t³ returns 73.13 km/s/Mpc — 0.2σ from SH0ES, 11.5σ from Planck — and predicts Ωm = 0.267, independently checkable against weak lensing, clusters, and BAO. [OPEN] Because ρΛ falls faster than matter running the clock backward, the temporal term dominates every earlier epoch rather than less of them — the opposite of what structure formation needs, a live tension raised here rather than papered over. The w = p/2−1 mapping survives a background-assumption challenge: redone self-dominated, both readings agree only at p = 0 and p = 3, which is exactly where the framework's own candidates sit. Route 2 in numbers · which part of an atom can see α move? —

A changing α shifts every atomic line by the same common factor (K = 2), so only the departure K−2 — the proper-time part of Module VIII's atom — can witness it: 3.3×10−5 of Lyman-α in hydrogen, 0.43 in U91+. Optical-clock bounds on α̇/α translate to |S| < 4.4×10−8 on the framework's coupling — seven decades below what "t₃ sets α" would require — so whatever sets α is proper time, consistent with how Module VIII already builds the atom. Separately, read as a plain Friedmann fluid, ρΛ ∝ t−3 gives w = +½, decelerating, not dark energy at all. The fix is to read t³ as the temporal volume t₁t₂t₃ rather than age cubed: letting nc of the three dimensions track cosmic age turns p from a dial into a count, w = nc/2−1, and the observed w = −1.03±0.03 inverts to nc = −0.06 ± 0.06 — a static temporal volume, the only count within reach by seventeen sigma over the next one.
